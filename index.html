    How rules scale
    • Over 30 days, each person needs 25 m³ of living volume (NASA HRP 2014 minimum for long missions). Shorter missions use an estimate of 10 m³.
    • Over 30 days, everyone gets private quarters of 5.4 m³ (NASA HRP 2014).
    • Exercise (17.25 m³ for 2 machines), hygiene (4.34 m³ per compartment, 2 compartments for >4 crew), eating (11.26 m³ per 4 crew) and logistics storage come from NASA's 380-day deep-space habitat study (Rucker 2012). Volumes are turned into floor area using the deck height.
    • Work and medical areas are estimates. NASA sizes work for up to 4 people at once and lets medical share the exercise area.
    • NASA-STD-3001 (V2 8014) requires escape routes free of stowed items. NASA sets no exit count or distance, so "2 exits for >4 crew or >90 days" and the 15 m / 12 m limits are design rules.
    • Hygiene must be kept apart from food prep (NASA-STD-3001 V2 8001) and away from crew quarters (NASA HRP 2014).
    • Serious safety problems cap the total score: no exit 30, no sleep quarters 40, trapped crew 45, hearing hazard or under half the volume 50, under half the sleep space 60, missing second exit 65.
    • Sleep noise limit tightens from NC-50 (≈58 dBA) to NC-40 (≈49 dBA) for missions over 30 days.

    Noise limits are from NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 2: sleep NC-40 for >30-day missions (V2 6079), work areas NC-50 (V2 6078), hazard <85 dBA anywhere (V2 6077). NC→dBA conversions (NC-40 ≈ 49, NC-50 ≈ 58 dBA) are from NASA's Shuttle acoustics paper. Medical uses the NC-50 work-area limit. The loudness of each zone (exercise 65 dBA etc.) is an estimate.

    Each rule above shows its source: blue links are NASA documents, gold tags are estimates or design rules. Tune them in getRules() and NASA_VOL.
